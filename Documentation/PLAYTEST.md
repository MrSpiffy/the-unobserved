# Version 0.7 — illusion stress test

Before the blind playtest below, deliberately try to catch a pop. Record wing, object/state, FOV, camera position, rotation direction and whether the object was partly occluded.

- Slowly sweep horizontally, vertically and diagonally past bridge supports, stairs, blockers, gallery apertures and decorative props. Check the final edge at both sides and top/bottom of the display, including wide FOV.
- Watch through an opening and from behind a narrow pillar. Partial coverage must retain observation. Hide an entire target behind a wide solid wall and confirm it can still change.
- Look back and forth around each decorative slot. Confirm distinct remembered silhouettes, no unseen background reroll, reasonable scale and unobstructed nearby signs/panels. Stand in a slot and check that it does not change around you.
- Keep one part of a floor visible while turning. Confirm the complete landing keeps its finish. Look upward or leave the room and return; distinguish tile, concrete and metal without mixed tile patches.
- Repeat both hero reveals with slow turns; check the union of the old/new room structures rather than just the central object.
- Complete all circuits at normal walking speed. Listen for excessive decorative audio, examine daylight exit, and record whether changes felt memorable or noisy.

Automated sweeps and progression checks are useful regressions, but do not replace these subjective and multi-FOV checks.

# Focused first-time playtest — 0.5.0

Use a fresh session of the built player. Prefer someone who has not seen the solution guide. Let them speak their reasoning without coaching; record interventions separately. Preserve the current speed, route and circuit count while gathering evidence.

| Section | Observe | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Reception/archive | First recognition that looking away caused a change; reading a cabinet as a blocker | Time of discovery, mistaken explanations, aimless turns |
| Atrium | Recognition of the garden replacement; understanding the two traversal branches | What they remember changing, missed route, falls |
| Workshop | Understanding hold/release; using the stair to reach Rise | Unnecessary rerolls, missed elevated panel, release decisions |
| Reservoir | Reusing the single anchor after a circuit latches | Whether persistence is understood, needless backtracking |
| Observatory | Coordinating two holds versus merely repeating a checklist | Time per alignment, confusion versus boredom |
| Gallery | Recognizing and aiming at an absence; crossing the vestibule | Failed E attempts, clarity of the transformed aperture |
| Engine/outside | Reclaiming holds for Return; satisfaction of departure | Whether they solve without help and recognize completion |

For each wing record entry/exit time, falls, hints given and one short player quote. Total first-time duration should be measured rather than inferred from the scripted integration run. The 15–30 minute target is not a reason to slow travel or add waiting.

Afterward ask: Which place changed most convincingly? Which object looked least like it belonged? When did you understand anchoring? Which puzzle felt repetitive? Did sound support the space or call too much attention to the mechanism?

Prioritize progression blocks and unreadable interactions first. If Observatory is repetitive, replace a redundant configuration with a more meaningful use of existing sightlines/holds; do not append another objective. If the first hero is unnoticed, adjust its reveal viewpoint/composition before adding more transformations. If the run is short but satisfying, do not pad it solely to reach a numerical duration.
