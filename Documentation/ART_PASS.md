# Environment art review — 0.6

## Highest-impact findings

The previous implementation separated collision from visual forms successfully, but much of that visual construction still comprised primitive boxes and cylinders. Palette swaps could dominate architectural identity. Reception repeated identical benches, structural platforms lacked convincing edges, and some large silhouettes remained unarticulated. The imported first pass also exposed floating light fixtures and distorted support meshes.

## Focused replacements

- Reception and archive: modeled counter, waiting group, cabinets and consistent civic wall elevations.
- Atrium: window bays, columns, real stair meshes and planted garden; the existing garden-to-service-core reveal gains actual doors and ventilation.
- Workshop and reservoir: machinery, desks, hopper vessels, plumbing and service elevations; platforms gain visible thickness and framed edges.
- Observatory: architectural columns and instrument-housing cladding support its existing sightline puzzle.
- Gallery and engine: familiar furniture becomes exhibits; a modeled recessed portal and machinery establish distinct identities. Selected late forms deliberately retain simplified construction.
- Functional forms: stairs, machinery/cabinet blockers, supported islands and framed crossings replace abstract visual prototypes. Collision, rise, span, anchor progression and route dimensions remain authored independently.

Ceiling fixtures meet their mounting surface; bridge beams now use the long axis of a column instead of stretching a thin support panel. Decorative object-name labels are removed while panel requirements and navigation signs remain. Imported structural surfaces participate in unseen revisions; furniture retains its material identity.

## Scope and limitations

No new objectives, corridor extensions, movement slowdown or major systems. Existing sparse audio and quiet form-specific transitions are preserved. The three realities are not three wholly rebuilt versions of every room: architectural form substitutions and two hero reveals provide stronger changes, with quieter surface revisions between them. This is a curated stylized environment, with some retained code-built shell and puzzle controls.

Automated progression and camera captures test implementation, not first-time puzzle comprehension or enjoyment. The user's experienced completion was approximately 6–7 minutes; the 15–30 minute first-play target remains unverified. Blind playtesting and lower-end hardware profiling remain necessary.
