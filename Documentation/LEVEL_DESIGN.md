There are **19 interaction stations**: 17 circuits and two instrument pickups. Seven wing interlocks require every local station to be connected. Connected circuits and open doors latch; release never undoes completed work. Instrument acquisition is explicit and included in its wing's exit requirements. The final outdoor threshold checks all 17 departure circuits before recording the escape.

The archive and gallery use alternate-side doorways. The atrium/reservoir/engine split into real branches over recessed voids. The workshop and observatory use occluding structures to make preservation meaningful. Connecting corridors turn around the institution, keeping sightlines and room identity separate.

Forms: Bridge is level; Steps rises 2.4m over ten treads; Pillar blocks; Islands require modest jumps; Empty keeps an architectural aperture/socket and supports occlusion-aware aiming through the logical reserve. State selection is bounded rather than pure luck. There is no wait after looking away.

The route intentionally allows some local alternate traversal choices, such as reaching an atrium platform from its return side. Both ventilation circuits are still required. Backspace is recovery, not a progression shortcut: it returns to the current wing's entrance.

The 16 peripheral filler reserves were removed in 0.5. Reception keeps its initial small furniture inconsistency. Larger contrast comes from two authored replacements: the atrium garden becomes a municipal service core; the first gallery opening gains a deep vestibule. Neither adds an objective or extends the route footprint, and neither repeatedly cycles. The gallery retains the same held-empty aperture and collision clearance.

Bridge, Steps, Pillar, Islands and Empty remain the five functional contracts. Their appearance can instead be institutional joinery/stone, structural concrete or service metalwork. The same requirement remains valid across appearances. Held entities keep both function and visual interpretation; the E prompt names the architectural object and its function.
